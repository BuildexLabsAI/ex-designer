# Top agencies ship brand systems, not books

A top agency's brand book for a major client is now a layered system published on the web, not a PDF. Its core has five parts: a one-page brand platform (purpose, audience, positioning, personality, values), a verbal identity, a visual identity, real applications with templates, and a governance block that names the owner, the approval rule and the version history. Big brands split access into a public trademark page, a partner hub and an internal site, and a machine-readable layer has appeared beside them: design tokens plus a short rules file that AI agents can load, with open formats but no proof yet that agents follow them. Agencies converge on one process (discover, define, express, apply, equip and govern) and develop strategy and visuals together rather than in sequence. For an existing brand the professional move is evidence first: protect the assets people already link to the brand, record them faithfully, and refresh only the expression layer unless the business needs more. Depth runs from a three-hour founder workshop to Wolff Olins's GSK programme, nearly two years and still running. Two cautions frame the rest: no agency or brand publishes a full internal brand book, so the chapter structure here is a synthesis; and in both sectors named in the question, house identities came from in-house teams, creative directors and specialist studios, while New York agencies appear mainly in bounded jobs such as e-commerce, motion and naming.

**How to read the evidence.** Untagged citations rest on pages the researchers opened and read. **[snippet]** marks a finding seen only in a search-result summary; treat it as unverified. Paragraphs that open with **Inference**, and the status columns in the tables, are synthesis rather than sourced fact. Frontify and Standards are vendors that sell the formats they describe.

## Five layers make up the deliverable, and public guides show only the visual tip

**Agencies hand over far more than the public sees.** Gretel (New York) ends every project by building and handing over full brand guidelines and assets, then runs workshops for in-house and outside partners ([Creative Boom](https://www.creativeboom.com/inspiration/dylan-mulvaney-of-gretel/)). Wolff Olins's GSK scope ran from a strategic framework through the visual system, motion, sound and scientific drawing, and ended with a toolkit for internal rollout ([Brand New transcript](https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/wp/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/BN_Podcast_Transcript_Ep_056.pdf)). Interbrand and Landor list the same layers plus messaging, sonic identity, naming, training and templates ([Interbrand](https://interbrand.com/services/identity/) [snippet]; [Landor](https://landor.com/en/our-work/opella/) [snippet]).

**Published guidelines are a narrower genre.** Across eight public visual guides, the logo appears in **8 of 8**, colour in 7, typography in 6, an introduction and partner or legal rules in 5 each, imagery in 3, voice in 2 and a strategy chapter in only 1 ([GOV.UK](https://brand.design-system.service.gov.uk/); [Wikimedia](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Brand); [NASA 1976](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/nasa_graphics_manual_nhb_1430-2_jan_1976.pdf); [Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/about/brand-assets/); [Spotify](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/design); [Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/brand-assets); [Dropbox](https://brand.dropbox.com/) [snippet]; [Uber](https://www.uber.com/careers/design/work/2018-redesign) [snippet]). GOV.UK's order is typical: Introduction, Graphic device, Logo system, Colour, Typography, Data, Brand in use. Verbal identity often gets its own book: Mailchimp's Content Style Guide covers voice and tone, grammar, accessibility, translation and a word list, and ends on a TL;DR chapter ([Mailchimp style guide](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/)).

**Length works against use.** SomeOne co-founder Simon Manchipp describes 100–200-page PDFs that cost a lot, are rarely read and consist mostly of prohibitions ([SomeOne](https://someoneinlondon.com/updates/people-dont-read-traditional-brand-guidelines-so-lets-stop-being-traditional)). Harbour's Grant Parker warns that rigid guidelines become a straitjacket that breaks as work moves between formats, from billboards to social feeds ([Creative Review](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/brand-guidelines-fit-for-purpose/), excerpt only).

**Inference.** Strategy, messaging and the full voice live in internal documents, which is why public guides rarely show them. The internal brand book and the external usage page are two genres, and every deliverable should say which one it is. The table merges the verified tables of contents with the agency deliverable lists. Optional chapters follow the brand types in the cases: motion and icons at Dropbox and Uber, data at GOV.UK, attribution at Spotify and Wikimedia, signage and vehicles at NASA, and one guideline set per product family at Google ([Partner Marketing Hub](https://partnermarketinghub.withgoogle.com/brands/google/overview/)).

| Chapter | What it holds | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Brand on a page | Scope, who the book is for, one-screen summary | Essential |
| 2. Brand platform | Purpose, audience, positioning, personality, values, in the owner's approved words | Essential (internal book) |
| 3. Verbal identity | 3–5 voice traits, tone by context, word list, do/don't pairs from real copy, naming rules | Essential |
| 4. Logo | Versions, clear space, minimum size, misuse | Essential |
| 5. Colour | Exact values, named roles, contrast pairs | Essential |
| 6. Typography | Faces, hierarchy, licensed fonts and their fallbacks | Essential |
| 7. Imagery | Photography, illustration or icons, whichever the brand uses; what to show and avoid | Essential |
| 8. Brand in use | Real examples and templates for the main touchpoints | Essential |
| 9. Governance and assets | Owner, contact, approval rule, downloads, version log | Essential |
| By trigger | Motion, iconography, data visualisation, accessibility, attribution, sub-brands, environments, sound, localisation, measurement | Optional |

## The website replaced the PDF, and a machine layer now sits beside it

**The move to the web is now behaviour, not just vendor talk.** GOV.UK removed its PDF and kept only an accessible web version on **24 March 2026** ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/govuk-brand-guidelines)). VSA Partners argues that PDFs are dense, hard to navigate and soon out of date, while a site forces a clearer hierarchy and updates at once ([VSA Partners](https://vsapartners.com/news-ideas/how-to-create-brand-guidelines-people-will-use)). Big brands tier access by audience: Google gives the public the basics, signs co-branding partners into a hub and sends its agencies to a separate site on request ([Partner Marketing Hub](https://partnermarketinghub.withgoogle.com/brands/google/overview/)), and IBM keeps its Brand Center behind an IBM login ([IBM Brand Center](https://www.ibm.com/brand/)).

**Governance fits on one screen.** The most complete example found, the University of Warwick's, requires brand-team approval for every asset at least **five working days** before publication, names a contact and an asset bank, and presents the brand team as a partner, not an enforcer ([Warwick](https://brand.warwick.ac.uk/brand-governance/)). GOV.UK names a team email and a GitHub repository and keeps a dated update log; Google gives each page an audit cycle with an expiry date; IBM's workplace guidelines link each page to its GitHub source ([GOV.UK guidelines](https://brand.design-system.service.gov.uk/); [IBM Workplace Design](https://www.ibm.com/design/workplace/brand-expression/overview)). The habit is old: NASA's 1976 manual opened with the Administrator's letter making it official policy, plus an index of the pages in effect on a given date ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/nasa_graphics_manual_nhb_1430-2_jan_1976.pdf)).

**AI clients get the system as tools.** Geist built Anthropic a UI component system and a modular CMS alongside the identity ([Geist](https://geist.co/work/anthropic)). Pentagram gave Cohere a Figma plugin and a custom Cinema 4D tool ([Pentagram](https://www.pentagram.com/work/cohere/story)), someform and MultiAdaptor built Google DeepMind a standalone app for on-brand 3D imagery ([Stash Media](https://www.stashmedia.tv/deep-inside-the-deepmind-rebrand-with-someform-studio/)), and Animade delivered the Meta AI ring as **25 Lottie animations (lightweight animation files) and 16 voice states** with system guidelines ([Animade](https://archive.animade.tv/work/meta-ai-ring)).

**The machine-readable layer has open formats but no proof of effect.** Agent Skills, an open format originally developed by Anthropic, packages instructions for an AI agent in a SKILL.md file ([agentskills.io](https://agentskills.io/)). Anthropic's own brand-guidelines skill fits on a screen: four neutrals and three accents with hex values and roles (dark #141413, light #faf9f5, orange #d97757 as the main accent), Poppins with an Arial fallback for headings of 24pt and up, Lora with a Georgia fallback for body text, and nothing on logo, voice or imagery ([SKILL.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthropics/skills/main/skills/brand-guidelines/SKILL.md)). Google's DESIGN.md, still alpha, pairs binding token values (colours, type, spacing) with prose sections in a fixed order, and its command-line tool checks contrast and exports to W3C design tokens (DTCG) and Tailwind ([DESIGN.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-labs-code/design.md/main/README.md)). Standards and Frontify serve guidelines to Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor and similar tools over MCP, the protocol AI tools use to fetch outside context; Frontify's server is off by default and warns that AI output still needs review ([Standards](https://help.standards.site/ai-mcp.md); [Frontify MCP](https://help.frontify.com/en/articles/14787214-frontify-mcp-beta)). Frontify itself calls machine rules a second document type, made of short rules with only one possible reading, not a replacement for the human book ([Frontify guide](https://www.frontify.com/en/guide/brand-guidelines-for-ai)). The researchers found no source that measures whether agents follow these files, and no published spec for "brand.md".

**Inference.** The 2026 default is one canonical URL with a visible last-updated date and a changelog; a PDF is at most an export. The machine layer's main risk is drift, so token and rules files should be generated from the brand's single token source, not kept by hand.

| Delivery layer | Audience | Holds | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Internal brand site | Staff, in-house and agency teams | The full book | Essential |
| Public usage page | Press, partners, developers | Logo files, misuse, permissions, trademark terms, contact | Essential once outsiders use the marks |
| Token file and rules file | AI agents, developers | Exact values and short do/don't rules, linked to the site | Essential for tech and AI; cheap for all |
| Partner hub, production tools, MCP server, PDF export | Partners, designers, agents | Gated assets, plugins, component libraries, live feed | Optional, by scale |

## One five-phase process scales from a three-hour sprint to a two-year programme

**Published accounts converge on five phases.** Gretel runs Discovery (ending in a findings presentation), Strategy (a framework that guides visual and verbal expression), Lab (sketching and iteration on both), Application (templates and tools) and Support (guidelines and partner workshops) ([Creative Boom](https://www.creativeboom.com/inspiration/dylan-mulvaney-of-gretel/)). Alina Wheeler's textbook follows the same arc and ends in launching and building brand champions ([Wiley-VCH](https://www.wiley-vch.de/en/areas-interest/art-culture/designing-brand-identity-978-1-119-98481-8) [snippet]), and Interbrand's strategy work opens with brand audits ([Interbrand](https://interbrand.com/services/strategy/) [snippet]). The rhetoric differs (Brian Collins frames the work as problem-seeking alongside problem-solving, [Eye](https://www.eyemagazine.com/feature/article/reputations-brian-collins); Michael Bierut as listening to the client and reading everything relevant, [kottke.org](https://kottke.org/06/09/how-design-works)), but the substance does not.

**Wolff Olins refuses to sign off strategy as words alone.** On GSK the team defined purpose, personality and tone first, explored three creative territories, ran hundreds of logo explorations, tested routes on research respondents and checked trademarks and meaning in every market. Emma Barratt, its global executive creative director, said "strategy is nothing without visuals attached to it" ([Brand New transcript](https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/wp/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/BN_Podcast_Transcript_Ep_056.pdf)). Discovery there meant working with product, business-unit and commercial heads while the CEO set the ambition; Interbrand's Banamex project involved staff, board, partners and clients at almost every step ([Interbrand](https://interbrand.com/articles/banamex-brand-strategy-identity/) [snippet]). No top agency publishes an interview count or a standard question list.

**Depth is documented only at the two ends.** Jake Knapp's Brand Sprint condenses strategy into six exercises over three hours: a 20-year roadmap; what, how and why; top values; top audiences; personality sliders; the competitive landscape ([Follow the Fox](https://followthefox.substack.com/p/how-a-brand-sprint-led-to-something), a secondary account of GV's original). Red Antler, with about half its clients pre-launch, involves the people who decide at every step ([WeWork](https://www.wework.com/ideas/worklife/what-emily-heyward-brand-guru-and-cofounder-of-red-antler-does-all-day) [snippet]). GSK had run for 18 months to nearly two years and was still going, with illustrators, scientists, AI specialists, a motion team, F37's Rick Banks on the logo and local partners in each market (transcript above). Wolff Olins rolled Uber's 2018 identity out to more than 80 countries with trainings and a creative-review process ([Uber](https://www.uber.com/careers/design/work/2018-redesign) [snippet]). Fees are rarely public; GOV.UK's refresh by M+C Saatchi reportedly cost about **£532,000** ([Civil Service World](https://civilserviceworld.com/professions/article/gov-uk-brand-refresh-gds-lnet-contract) [snippet]). VSA Partners adds a rule that cuts across tiers: expert teams get principles, broad mixed-skill teams get firm rules ([VSA Partners](https://vsapartners.com/news-ideas/how-to-create-brand-guidelines-people-will-use)).

**Inference.** Both tables are composites: the phases merge the Gretel, Wolff Olins, Wheeler and Interbrand accounts, and the mid-size tier is interpolated between the two documented ends. Define and Express overlap in practice, so a platform should always be shown beside real visual and verbal examples.

| Phase | Work | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Discover | Owner and leadership interviews, background reading, customer input, audit of own and rival materials | Findings presentation or diagnostic |
| Define | Purpose, positioning, personality and tone, boiled down to one idea | Brand platform |
| Express | Two or three territories, one route, refinement; enterprise adds route testing and trademark checks | Core visual and verbal system |
| Apply | Main touchpoints, templates, tools | Application set |
| Equip and govern | Guidelines, asset library, onboarding, ongoing guardianship | Brand site, assets, governance |

| Tier | Discovery and strategy | System | Delivery and governance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Startup | Founder workshop on values, audiences, personality, rivals | Logo, colour, type, imagery basics | Essentials guide, a few templates; founder approves |
| Mid-size | Leadership, staff and customer interviews; audit; brand platform | Full visual and verbal system | Web guidelines, templates, one onboarding session |
| Global enterprise | Adds route testing, brand architecture, trademark and language checks per market | Adds motion, sound, custom type, illustration programme | Portal and toolkit, training, local partners, agency as guardian |

## Existing brands get recorded faithfully first and refreshed second

**Professionals treat an existing brand as evidence.** Koto starts from the jobs the brand must do and quantifies its equity; it warns that cutting distinctive assets can be disastrous, that longevity is not proof of equity, and that a refresh should never launch while customers or shareholders are unhappy, though it admits its own default leans revolutionary ([Koto](https://offbrandkoto.substack.com/p/brand-evolution-or-revolution-knowing)). Lippincott's research on vital brands found evolutionary change: iconic logos stayed almost intact while illustration, palette and typography moved ([Lippincott](https://www.lippincott.com/ideas/the-fountain-of-brand-youth/) [snippet]). Wolff Olins kept GSK's name and set it in larger block capitals, dropping a symbol that shrank the name and did not work on screens (transcript above). Interbrand compressed LEGO's **23 guidelines and 110+ principles into five** ([Interbrand](https://interbrand.com/work/lego/) [snippet]).

**The distinctive-asset grid decides what to protect.** The Ehrenberg-Bass Institute rates each element on fame (the share of category buyers who link it, unbranded, to the brand) and uniqueness (how exclusively it points there). High on both means protect, famous but generic means sharpen, and unique but little known means invest ([Umbrex](https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/marketing-frameworks/distinctive-brand-assets-framework-ehrenberg-bass/) [snippet]). Jenni Romaniuk warns that prompted surveys inflate fame scores by up to about 20 points ([Ehrenberg-Bass](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/mumbrella360-ehrenberg-bass-professor-says-youre-poorly-branding-if-youre-focusing-on-assets-over-your-brand) [snippet]).

**Faithful documentation has a New York model.** Order (Brooklyn) reissued the 1970 New York City Transit Authority manual by Massimo Vignelli and Bob Noorda as unedited scans of the original pages, with a new foreword by Bierut and an essay added around them ([Order](https://order.design/project/nycta-graphics-standards-manual-reissue); [It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/vignelli-standards-manual)). The owner's words get the same care: Barratt wrote down the three adjectives GSK's CEO used at the pitch and replayed them whenever the work needed courage (transcript above). For voice, Mailchimp separates a stable voice from a tone that shifts with context ([Mailchimp](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/) [snippet]); no top agency publishes a method for deriving voice from existing copy.

**Fashion shows the price of breaking continuity.** Most fashion logo changes from 2017 to 2026 restored archive material: Celine's 1960s logo, Burberry's knight from about 1901, Calvin Klein's original spirit and DVF's 1970s Vignelli monogram ([It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/news/celine-logo-redesign-hedi-slimane-graphic-design-030918); [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2023/02/07/burberry-daniel-lee-logo-equestrian-knight-design/); [Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/6-12-february-2017/calvin-klein-unveils-new-logo-collaboration-peter-saville/) [snippet]; [FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.uk/news/fashion/diane-von-furstenberg-unveils-rebrand/2017011623159) [snippet]). Peter Saville called Burberry's 2023 reversal of his identity irresponsible and a failure of management, and argued that a house of that scale should not give up continuity, while praising the new design itself. He also admitted his 2018 system was never fully rolled out, so several versions circulated ([Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2025/06/13/burberry-logo-redesign-irresponsible-peter-saville/)).

**Inference.** The workflow for an existing brand: state the objective; inventory every asset in use; rate each for fame and uniqueness; decide keep, sharpen, invest or drop; record the kept assets exactly, with provenance (source, year, designer, confirmed or inferred); and refresh only the expression layer unless the objective needs more. Keep the faithful record and the recommendations in separate sections. The visual and verbal layers can be read from live output, but purpose, positioning and values cannot be reverse-engineered from a website; they need the owner's own words and sign-off. Without survey data, equity ratings are hypotheses and should say so.

## AI brands codify a product system while fashion houses guard a private image world

**Anthropic's public trail shows the AI stack.** Geist, a Portland studio, worked with the founders for about two and a half years before Claude's mid-2023 launch, delivering a typographic wordmark, site messaging, art direction, an illustration language built on human values, documentary photography by Aaron Wojack, Styrene and Tiempos type, the UI component system and the CMS ([Geist](https://geist.co/work/anthropic)). An in-house Creative Studio now runs brand expression ([Accel job listing](https://jobs.accel.com/companies/anthropic/jobs/67669093-brand-designer-core-brand) [snippet]), and Mother London made the first paid campaign, Keep thinking ([Campaign](https://www.campaignlive.com/article/anthropic-launches-first-work-with-mother-for-ai-agent-claude/1933083) [snippet]). The public layer is legal: the Trademark Guidelines, effective 1 August 2024, ban any alteration of the marks, require prior approval and route partners to a marketing address ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/trademark-guidelines)). The machine layer is the skill described above, which names Poppins and Lora rather than the Styrene and Tiempos that Geist specified, without saying why.

**OpenAI and its peers keep the same split.** OpenAI's February 2025 refresh was led in-house, with ABC Dinamo drawing OpenAI Sans and Studio Dumbar/DEPT as motion partner, to fix an identity that had fragmented as teams grew ([Creative Review](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/openai-brand-refresh/)). Its public page bans "GPT" in other companies' product names and requires both brands to approve co-branded material ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/brand/) [snippet]). Mistral's public page is logo files, three do's and six don'ts, with no colour values or typography ([Mistral](https://mistral.ai/brand)).

**AI brands need two chapters other sectors lack.** Naming and attribution matter because third parties build on the product. Product voice is set by model-behaviour documents more than by marketing guides: Anthropic's constitution says Claude must never deny being an AI to someone who sincerely asks and must avoid sycophancy ([Claude's constitution](https://www.anthropic.com/constitution)), and Meta requires its AI chat to signal clearly that AI is present and where it falls short ([Meta](https://www.meta.com/design-at-meta/blog/4-principles-for-designing-AI-products/)). Imagery steers away from cliché: DeepMind commissions artists to replace glowing brains, code streams and white robots ([DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/discover/visualising-ai) [snippet]), and OpenAI pairs commissioned photography with Sora-generated textures ([Creative Review](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/openai-brand-refresh/)).

**Fashion houses publish no brand book at all.** Identity lives in the creative director's house codes, a closed roster of photographers ([The Impression on Saint Laurent](https://theimpression.com/saint-laurent-ad-campaign-archive-2017-to-2021-anthony-vaccarello/) [snippet]), formal colour standards such as Pantone's kate spade green ([FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.uk/news/fashion/kate-spade-celebrates-30th-anniversary-with-pantone-collaboration/2023020667723) [snippet]) and seasonal store-presentation guidelines ([Ralph Lauren careers](https://careers.ralphlauren.com/CareersCorporate/JobDetail/Brand-Image-Manager-Creative-Presentation/52455) [snippet]). New identities launch by wiping the Instagram feed, as Celine did in 2018 and Burberry in 2023 ([It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/news/celine-logo-redesign-hedi-slimane-graphic-design-030918); [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2023/02/07/burberry-daniel-lee-logo-equestrian-knight-design/)). Outside agencies do bounded jobs: Work & Co built Celine's e-commerce, RIOT (New York) made the visual and motion identity for Coach's sub-brand Coachtopia, and Carbone Smolan named and branded the parent company Tapestry ([Media Marketing](https://www.media-marketing.com/en/news/paris-fashion-house-celine-makes-e-commerce-debut-on-sophisticated-minimalist-site/) [snippet]; [RIOT](https://riot.nyc/portfolio/coachtopia-logo-stings-motion-branding) [snippet]; [GDUSA](https://gdusa.com/new-name-and-optimistic-yellow-for-coach-inc/) [snippet]). The researchers verified no 2017–2026 fashion-house identity by Pentagram, Collins, Mother Design or Base Design.

**Inference.** An AI brand book adds naming and attribution, product self-reference rules, an imagery avoid-list, the UI component system and the machine layer; its machine file should say when free fonts stand in for licensed ones, as Poppins and Lora appear to do in Anthropic's skill. A fashion brand book stays private and image-led, governs through codes and approvals, and should mark which assets are permanent (name, core mark, signature colour or material) and which belong to the current creative director.

## Conclusion

The unit of delivery is no longer the book but the division of labour between layers. The craft is deciding where each rule lives: legal terms on the public page, the reasons and the full system on the internal site, exact values in one token source that every other layer is generated from, and taste in principles rather than rules. Perplexity shows why the last part matters: its team kept the guidelines flexible, and none of the posters featured in a 2024 profile of the brand came from them ([Off Menu](https://offmenu.substack.com/p/perplexity-the-ai-brand-everyone)).

The professional bar is usable and exact, not long, and it has two tests: people stop keeping private versions (67% of enterprise respondents did in a 2021 Frontify survey, [BusinessWire](https://businesswire.com/news/home/20210422005337/en/New-Study-From-Frontify-Finds-Most-Brands-Struggle-Due-to-Lack-of-Team-Inclusion-and-Brand-Ownership) [snippet]), and the rules survive formats nobody planned for. The open questions are exactly the ones vendors fill with assertion: there is no neutral data on how guidelines get used or on whether AI agents obey machine-readable brand files, and no published source backs any chapter or page count as the standard.
